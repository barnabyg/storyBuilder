Kari Lund knelt beside the supply sled outside the cabin, pressing her split thumbnail against each ice pick's strap. Her patched orange jacket caught beneath her knee.

"Picks. Probing pole. Throw line," Ingrid read.

Kari's older sister stood over her in blue waterproofs, a brass compass hanging from her neck.

"I guide winter trips for a living," Kari said. "You can stop watching my hands."

"Spare clothes."

Kari opened the bundle, checked socks and layers, then rolled it tight. She extended the pole and checked its joints. She uncoiled the throw line, checked its length, and packed it again. Finally she pulled the low sled forward and tested its lashings.

Ingrid unfolded the map beneath her list. A photograph clipped inside showed their father, Erik, wearing a red hat above his square beard.

Kari glanced at the date.

"That's an old winter."

"I can read it."

Erik had called Ingrid sensible and Kari fearless. Kari had been twelve when she first asked why she couldn't be both. He had handed her an axe and told her to fetch wood.

She bent over the sled again.

The screw-top tin sat inside the waterproof bag. Kari checked its lid. Ingrid held the bag while Kari closed it, then they secured it beneath the straps.

Ingrid touched the far shore on the map.

"He asked us to scatter him here."

"Three kilometres."

"After several days of thaw," Ingrid said. "We agree before we leave. If our tests can't establish that the ice is safe, we turn back."

Kari tightened a strap.

"Kari."

"All right. We turn back."

Ingrid went inside and laid dry kindling beside the stove. Their preparations had taken fifteen minutes when she returned and closed the door without locking it.

Kari stepped onto the lake first. She drove her probe into the ice ahead, then moved to the tested spot. The sled followed on a loose haul line. Its runners barely sank into the snow.

"Keep ten metres between us," she said.

Ingrid waited, then followed in her tracks.

They worked outward in short stretches. Kari tested each patch where the snow changed. Behind them, the cabin remained visible between the trees.

Ingrid stopped twice to tighten her right snowshoe. The third time, her boot slipped sideways.

"Wait."

Kari stopped the sled and went back along their tracks. Ingrid lifted her foot and held it still.

"The strap keeps coming loose."

Kari pulled off a glove. With her split thumbnail, she picked ice from the buckle.

Ingrid watched her mouth.

"Go on," Ingrid said.

"What?"

"Say it."

Kari threaded the strap through again and pulled it tight.

"Put your weight on it."

Ingrid stamped once, then took a step.

"Better. Thank you."

Kari put her glove back on and returned to the sled.

Twenty-five minutes after leaving the cabin, they had covered about five hundred metres. Ingrid checked their position against the shore and unfolded Erik's map.

"He marked this section thick clear ice."

Kari scraped snow aside with her boot. Beneath it lay a crust. Her probe broke through that, and water filled the hole.

She cleared a wider patch. The ice below was cloudy.

Ingrid stayed back.

"We agreed. Let's turn around."

Kari looked to their left. A strip of exposed ice lay less than twenty metres away.

"That's clear. We can test it."

"There's water here."

"I see it. Let me test that strip before we give up the crossing."

Ingrid folded the map.

Kari pointed along the exposed strip of ice.

"We can check that way. Stay clear of the wet snow."

She led with the probing pole, striking ahead before each step. Ingrid followed with the sled, leaving space between them. Where snow covered the strip, Kari scraped it aside with her boot and tested again.

They moved two hundred metres in thirteen minutes. Kari checked her watch, then stopped where a seam crossed their path beneath a drift. Beyond it, bare ice continued toward the far shore.

She pushed the pole into the snow. Its tip broke through with a splash. Kari pulled it back and crouched without moving her feet. Water filled the hole.

"We go back," Ingrid said. "That was our agreement."

"I can see it."

"Then stop looking for a way over."

Kari stood. She gripped the pole with both hands.

"You wanted this crossing. You wanted to say we finished his journey."

"I wanted us to try."

"You want the credit without taking any risk. You get there and tell everyone you brought him across."

Ingrid set down the sled rope.

"Your shortcut advice already helped kill him. I'm not following you onto another one."

Kari pressed her split thumbnail against the pole until it hurt.

"I told him where I'd crossed. I didn't tell him the ice would hold forever."

"You told him it was safe."

"And you let him cross alone."

"He asked me. I had work."

"You could have gone."

Fifteen minutes had passed since Ingrid put away the map. Kari lifted the pole clear of the hole.

"Fine. Back."

She turned sharply toward their outward track, away from the far shore. Ingrid picked up the rope behind her.

Her next step landed beyond the marks from her probing pole. The ice broke beneath one snowshoe.

Kari dropped to her hips. Water flooded her trousers. She flung her arms wide and caught the edge with her forearms. Behind her, the sled stopped on firm ice, its haul line slack.

She pulled the picks from her jacket and drove their points into the surface. Her first breath came with a cry.

"Stay back."

Ingrid was already lowering herself. She lay on her stomach well behind the break and threw the coiled rescue line. It landed short.

Kari held with one pick and reached. Her glove closed on nothing.

"Again."

Ingrid drew it back and threw. This time the loop struck Kari's wrist. She pushed her arm through and gripped the rope.

Her snowshoe held beneath the edge. She pulled once, hard, and water rose over her waist.

"Don't pull yet."

Ingrid spread her legs and pressed her boots against the ice.

Kari kicked backward. The binding held. She kicked again, turning her foot until her heel slipped out. Her boot stayed on.

"Now."

Ingrid drew the rope toward her chest. Kari dug in both picks and kicked until her stomach slid over the edge. She dragged her knees clear, then flattened herself.

Two and a half minutes had passed before she was fully out.

She crawled toward their tested track, dragging the rope. Ingrid moved backward on her stomach, keeping it taut.

Only when Kari reached their marks did Ingrid take the sled's haul line. Still lying down, she pulled it toward them in short lengths.

They spent another minute and a half moving back along the track. Ingrid stopped beside Kari and opened the sled's bag.

Ingrid pulled out the shelter sheet and held it around Kari on the ice they had tested. Kari crouched behind it, unfastening her boots with fingers she could barely bend.

"Hold that corner lower," she said.

Ingrid lowered it.

Getting out of the trousers took both hands. Kari peeled away her soaked lower layers, then worked each sock over her heel. She sat on the dry bag to pull on the spares.

"I wanted to finish it," Kari said. "To prove my judgement hadn't killed Erik."

Ingrid kept the sheet stretched between her hands.

"I told him about the shortcut. I should have made sure he understood when I'd last checked it." Kari stopped to grip her ankle. "But recommending a route wasn't choosing it for him. He chose to go."

She pulled a dry sock over her foot.

"And today I kept looking for a way through after we agreed to turn back. That was my decision."

Eight minutes passed before she had replaced her lower layers and socks and fastened her boots. Ingrid held the sheet while Kari stood, then helped her close the bag around the wet clothes.

"I said no when he asked me," Ingrid said. "Blaming you meant I didn't have to think about that."

Kari pressed her hands beneath her arms.

"I'm sorry I treated your work like you were still a child taking chances," Ingrid said. She folded the sheet.

Kari tried a few steps. Her legs shook, but held.

"We're going back to the cabin. Will you carry the map? I'll check our old track."

Ingrid put the map inside her jacket. Twelve minutes after opening the bag, she closed it. Kari took up the probe and turned toward their footprints.

Kari set the probe into their outward tracks. She tested the ice before each step, even where their boot marks showed.

Ingrid followed with the sled, keeping the rope between them clear. At the first stop, Kari held out her hand for the sled traces. Ingrid passed them over and took the probe.

They changed again twice. Neither asked who should lead. When Kari stopped to rest, Ingrid waited without touching her.

The seven hundred metres back to shore took thirty minutes. They pulled the sled up beside the cabin steps.

Inside, Ingrid struck a match and lit the kindling laid in the stove. Kari unfastened her patched orange jacket. The cuff caught on her split thumbnail, and she eased it free.

She hung her damp outer gear by the door, changed her remaining wet clothes, and wrapped herself in a blanket. Ingrid put water on the stove, then brought a chair closer for her.

Kari sat with her feet on a folded sack. When Ingrid handed her a mug of tea, she held it in both hands.

They had been inside twenty-five minutes before Ingrid opened the waterproof bag. She took out the tin and set it between their mugs.

"I can't put another crossing on the calendar," she said. "Not another test of which of us is right."

Kari put down her tea.

"The rock ledge beside the cabin," she said. "Where he taught us rescue knots."

"He asked for the far shore."

"I know. We could scatter them here."

Ingrid turned the tin once beneath her palm.

"On the ledge," she said. "Together."

"When we're ready."

Ingrid nodded and moved the tin away from the edge of the table. Kari took another drink.

Kari finished her drink and stood beside the stove for three minutes. Her hands had stopped shaking. She fastened her orange jacket and picked up the tin.

"Ready?" Ingrid asked.

Kari nodded.

They took the cleared path behind the cabin, walking slowly where snow covered the gravel. At the ledge, Kari tested her footing before stepping nearer the edge. The far shore was visible across the lake. She could make out trees, but not the place where their father had meant them to land.

Ingrid held out her hand for the tin.

She unscrewed the lid. Kari steadied the base, her split thumbnail pressed against the metal. They stood together for a minute, watching the breeze lift snow from the ledge.

"Now," Kari said.

They tipped the tin together. Ash fell beyond the rock, then spread over the lake in the breeze. Some settled on the snow below. They waited, then tipped it farther until it was empty. Ingrid put the lid back on.

"I'm sorry I said you let Dad die."

Ingrid took her hand.

"I should never have made you carry his decision alone."

Kari held on. After a while, they turned back along the path.

At the cabin door, Ingrid unfolded Erik's map against the wall. Kari took the pencil from its fold. She wrote the date beside the place where she had broken through.

"Weak seam," she said, writing. "Do not cross."

Ingrid held the paper flat while Kari finished.

"We'll keep this," Ingrid said. "Not with the things we're dividing."

"Inside," Kari said.

Through the doorway, the clock showed twelve minutes since they had left the table. Each holding an edge, they carried the map inside.
